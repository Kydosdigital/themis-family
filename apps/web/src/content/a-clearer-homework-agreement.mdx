“Finish your homework before you play” sounds clear. Until one person means “start it”, another means “finish every question” and nobody has talked about who checks.

A useful agreement makes those missing details visible before the evening gets busy.

## Name the task, not just the category

“Homework” can mean three worksheets, ten minutes of reading or an assignment that needs a conversation. Start with what is actually expected today.

An agreement might be: “Finish the maths questions and show me before 6 PM.” It gives both people something specific to refer to.

## Agree what happens next

Talk about the consequence before it is needed. If certain games pause until the work has been approved, say which games. Explain what stays available.

The aim is a shared understanding, not a surprise at six o’clock.

## Leave room for the thing you didn’t predict

Perhaps the work takes longer than expected. Perhaps an instruction makes no sense. A way to ask for help or more time belongs in the plan.

- What can your child do if they get stuck?
- Who can check the work?
- What happens if that adult is busy?
- Which school tools should remain available?

## Separate finishing from approval

A child saying “I’m done” and a parent confirming it are two different steps. Make the waiting state clear, too.

Themis is designed around that distinction. It does not automatically judge homework or chores. Supported in-app session verification is a separate capability.

## Revisit the agreement

A routine that made sense last term may need changing. An agreement can be consistent without being permanent.

The technology can support the boundary. The family still decides what a reasonable boundary is.
