Before discussing when a phone should pause, it helps to discuss what a parental-control tool actually makes visible.

A teen may hear “digital boundaries” and worry about private conversations, friendships or being watched throughout the day. Those are worth addressing directly.

## Ask what they think the tool can see

The answer may be very different from the product’s actual behaviour. Start by listening, then look at the privacy information together.

Useful questions include:

- Can anyone read message contents?
- Is there a browsing-history feed?
- Is location tracked continuously?
- Can I see when controls are active?
- What information is included in parent summaries?

## Explain the reporting, not just the restrictions

Knowing that a game pauses at ten is only part of the picture. A young person should also understand what records are created and who can see them.

Themis’s intended summaries concern agreements, tasks, requests and supported sessions. They are not designed as a minute-by-minute surveillance feed.

## Make the limits easy to find

Privacy information should not be a promise hidden on a marketing page. It needs to be understandable in the child and teen experience itself.

Themis’s requirements include a visible indication that controls are active and an explanation of what parents can and cannot see.

## Keep checking your assumptions

A product’s permissions, documentation and actual behaviour should agree. Where a capability is still being verified, the honest answer is that it is not yet confirmed.

For Themis, Apple-controlled reporting and real-device enforcement remain subject to verification before public launch.

## Boundaries and dignity can share the same plan

Privacy is not something to discuss only after a disagreement. It can be part of the agreement from the beginning.
